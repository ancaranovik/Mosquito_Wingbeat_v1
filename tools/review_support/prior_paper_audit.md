# Audit of the prior Synthetic Swarm proof of concept

Audit date: 22 September 2026. This is supporting evidence for the revised proposal, not a revision of the earlier paper or a reimplementation of the experiment.

It records submission on 13 December 2025 and the comment “Accepted at RIVF 2025.” Citing arXiv:2512.12365 is valid. ## Additional source-code findings requiring reconciliation

These are properties of the available notebook, not verified descriptions of the run reported in the PDFs. Cell numbering here is zero-based; the extracted text marks cell boundaries.

- Cell 1 samples 3–7 requested chunks, up to three species, for a five-second output; source WAVs are peak-normalized before random crop selection. It does not check crop activity. The same WAV can be drawn repeatedly, and source-file identifiers are not written into the JSON provenance.
- Cell 1's `mix_chunks_separated` uses three gain centers (0.15, 0.5, 0.85), selection weights (0.3, 0.4, 0.3), and uniform ±0.05 variation. Its first onset is zero and subsequent onsets are cumulative random delays of 0.3–1.2 s. Those settings differ from the manuscript's independent uniform 0–3 s offsets and uniform 0.2–1.0 gains. Overlap is possible, not guaranteed.
- Cell 1 sets `SNR_RANGE=(30,50)`, unlike the PDF's 20–40 dB. Its noise multiplier is `10**(-snr/20)` applied to unit Gaussian noise, without measuring the mixture RMS or scaling noise relative to that RMS. Therefore the number named SNR is not necessarily the achieved signal-to-noise ratio, especially for sparse mixtures.
- Cell 1 builds an eight-second buffer and truncates it to five seconds after constructing species counts. Later source events may be partly or entirely discarded while their species label survives. The new generator must derive labels from the final retained active events and keep onset/end/sample provenance. This is a code-path risk; no claim is made about how often it occurred in the released dataset.
- Cells 5 and 6 explicitly request `n_mels=128`, but omit `n_fft`, `win_length` and `hop_length`. The executed library version and actual defaults are not recorded here; the manuscript's specific 512/400/160 parameters cannot be established from those calls. `IMG_SIZE` is declared in feature conversion, but the plotting call does not itself enforce 224×224; the training transforms later resize images.
- The training cells split rendered mixture samples 80/20, then use 10% of the remaining training subset as validation (nominally 72/8/20), stratifying by label count rather than source identity. No `random_state` is passed, and repeated cells rerun the split. Any comparisons across models/threshold runs should therefore be reproduced with frozen grouped splits and paired seeds.
- Model heads return logits; `nn.BCEWithLogitsLoss()` consumes those logits; `torch.sigmoid` appears in metrics/inference. Describe the manuscript's loss problem as notation ambiguity only.

## Visual inspection of the supplied PDF

Pages 3, 4 and 5 were rendered locally; pages 3 and 5 were visually checked. Page 5's learning-curve figures show training accuracy approaching one while validation remains materially lower, and training loss decreasing while validation loss remains around 0.6–0.7. Those figures do not support the prose's strong claims of avoiding overfitting or consistently rising validation accuracy. Do not carry those claims forward. The p. 3 Fig. 2 caption calls vertical lines wingbeat frequencies; conventional spectrograms with horizontal time and vertical frequency show stable tonal components as horizontal ridges, while vertical structures indicate temporal broadband events. The figure omits axes, so the revised proposal should not infer physical wingbeat structure from that image alone.

## Suggested compact revision text

The earlier proof of concept provides a useful offline multi-label baseline, but its reported settings require reconciliation before exact reproduction. Both supplied versions report 16 kHz audio, randomly selected 0.3–0.6 s chunks, 224×224 rendered log-Mel inputs, and ResNet-18/recurrent models. They contain conflicting Mel-band counts (64/128) and source-count descriptions (1–10 versus 3–7), while the linked notebook differs in its gain, onset and noise settings. The revised study will freeze one documented reconstruction and label it as such; it will not claim exact reproduction until the experiment configuration is established. Original-source partitions and final-event provenance will be fixed before synthesis. Loss notation will distinguish logits from probabilities, and validation-selected thresholds will be applied to a fixed trained model. The paper's reference to 12 swarm audios is not reconciled with the three approximately 12 s recordings now available; no assumption about their relationship will be made, and no independent acoustic benchmark will be inferred from either description. These are natural reproducibility and validation improvements to a proof of concept, not grounds for discarding its central idea.
